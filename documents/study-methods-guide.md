## Effective Study Methods

### Active Recall

Test yourself on the material rather than passively re-reading. Use flashcards, practice questions, and self-quizzing to strengthen memory.

### Spaced Repetition

Review material at increasing intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days. This builds long-term retention more effectively than cramming.

### Cornell Note-Taking

Divide your page into three sections:
1. **Main notes** (right column): Record key ideas during the lecture or reading
2. **Cue column** (left column): Add questions and keywords after the session
3. **Summary** (bottom): Write a brief summary of the page

### Pomodoro Technique

Study in focused 25-minute blocks with 5-minute breaks. After four blocks, take a longer 15-30 minute break.

### Elaborative Interrogation

Ask "why" and "how" questions about the material. Connecting new information to existing knowledge improves understanding and retention.

### Interleaving

Mix different topics or problem types within a study session rather than focusing on one topic at a time. This improves your ability to distinguish between concepts.
